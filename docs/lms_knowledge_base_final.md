# Masai School LMS — Student Support Knowledge Base

This is the working knowledge base for answering student questions about the Masai School LMS — what's on the platform, how it works, and the policies behind it. A short list of points still pending final confirmation from Ops is at the end (§30); everything else here is safe to use as-is.

---

## 1. Platform Overview and Getting Started

The Masai School LMS is the central platform students use throughout their program, accessible at **students.masaischool.com**. It's where students attend lectures, complete assignments, access reading materials, view their schedule, track progress, communicate with peers and instructors, raise support tickets, and manage their account. A companion mobile app (Masai Learn App) is also available.

Masai runs several distinct partner programs (for example, a Specialized Program in AI/ML with Drone Tech via IIT Roorkee, an AI/ML certificate program via IIT Patna, a Minor in Cybersecurity & Ethical Hacking via IIT Mandi, and others). **Certification criteria, attendance requirements, and grading weightage are program-specific — never assume one program's numbers apply to another.** See §26 for a reference table of what's confirmed per program.

Students may be enrolled in one or more batches simultaneously and can switch between them on the LMS.

### Signing In and Account Access

Students sign in at students.masaischool.com with their registered email and password. A "Forgot your password?" link handles resets. Common login issues are usually a wrong email/password or a browser/cache issue — try the password reset link first, then a support ticket under **Product Support > LMS Learning Management System** if it persists.

### Navigating the Platform

Top navigation: **Home, Learn, Support, Discussions, Refer & Earn**. Top-right icons: mobile app download prompt, **Calendar**, **Chat**, **Announcements**, and the **Profile** dropdown (My Profile, My Courses, Bookmark, MasaiVerse Community, Practice Interviews, Report a Bug, Level Up, Switch to OLD LMS, Sign out).

## 2. Home Dashboard

The Home Dashboard shows a personalized greeting, a **Your Schedule** panel (upcoming sessions with date, time, batch code, session type, and module), a **Pending Tasks** panel (open assignments), a **Product Updates** FAQ sidebar, and **Announcements**.

## 3. Learn Section — Lectures

The **Learn** section has three tabs: **Lectures**, **Assignments**, and **Resources**. The Lectures tab lists all lectures in reverse chronological order, each showing the instructor, date/time, session-type tags (Academic-Lecture, Industry-Mentor-Lecture, Doubt-Resolution-Class, Program-Coordinator-Connect), priority (Recommended/Mandatory), module, and attendance status (Present/Absent). Lectures can be searched and filtered by module, category, progress, attendance status, date, priority, and instructor.

Each lecture's detail page has a video player (quality selector, 360p–1080p), an AI Tutor sidebar, and five tabs: Transcript, Description, AI Summary, AI Chat, and Discussions.

Attendance mechanics are covered in full in §20 — in short: live sessions require ~90% participation, recordings must be watched in full within 30 days, and only mandatory academic/faculty and Industry Mentor sessions count (tutorials, masterclasses, and Helpdesk sessions are excluded).

## 4. Learn Section — Assignments

Assignments are listed with instructor, time window, and tags for type and priority, and show a status of In Progress / Complete / not started.

**Two formats, by answer type:**

- **Objective** — MCQ-style, auto-graded, score appears right after submission.
- **Subjective** — written responses, generally reviewed and discussed in Tutorial/TA sessions rather than shown as an automatic score.

**Two formats, by purpose:**

- **Practice/Recommended assignments** — for learning and self-assessment. A student can keep attempting until the deadline (the last answer submitted before the deadline counts as final), and these usually stay open for practice even after the deadline, without penalty. They generally do **not** affect CGPA.
- **Graded/Mandatory assignments** — show a score so the student knows where they stand, but unless a program's specific grading policy says otherwise, most assignment grades still don't feed directly into CGPA — CGPA is mainly driven by projects and formal evaluations. Check the specific program's grading policy before telling a student either way, since this is a common point of confusion.

**Extensions:** the default answer is no — timelines are the same for everyone and generally not modified individually, even for genuine circumstances (health, work, exams). A few programs allow exceptions in "exceptional circumstances" with supporting documentation, reviewed case-by-case, but this isn't guaranteed. Practice assignments remain accessible after their deadline regardless.

**Submission format:** depends on the specific assignment's instructions (file type, size limit, or a GitHub/Drive link) — there's no single platform-wide file format or size rule, so always point students to that assignment's own instructions. If a link submission is required (GitHub/Drive), sharing permissions must allow "Anyone with the link can view." If uploads fail: clear cache/cookies or use an Incognito window, confirm the file matches the required format, and verify submission by checking for a "Submitted" status, a confirmation email, or re-downloading the uploaded file.

## 5. Learn Section — Resources

The Resources tab holds lecture notes, PPTs, notebooks, and datasets, organized by module/lecture and typically uploaded within about 48 hours of the live class. Materials are split into **Pre-Reads** (before a lecture) and **Notes** (after).

Resources are **view-only through the platform and cannot be downloaded or printed** — students need an active internet connection to review them; there is no offline copy.

## 6. Course Details

Accessed via **Learn > Course Details** or **Profile > My Courses**. Shows the program name, partner institution, a course-progress bar, a module timeline, and program staff (Industry Mentors, Teaching Assistants, Curriculum Coordinator, Program Coordinator).

Certification eligibility (minimum CGPA/score and attendance) is program-specific — see §26 for the confirmed reference table rather than quoting one number for every student.

Placement/IRP support eligibility, independent of the specific certification criteria, requires an **overall CGPA of 7.0 or above** across the program (one confirmed program also requires graduation status alongside the CGPA). Attendance **can** separately factor into placement eligibility depending on the program — confirmed example: one program ("Model 3") requires 65% attendance alongside a CGPA of 7 for placement eligibility. Always check the specific program rather than assuming attendance is or isn't part of it.

There is also a separate, **monthly attendance certificate**, issued only to students who achieve **100% attendance** for that month — distinct from the end-of-program completion certificate. This is a real, confirmed document (resolves an earlier internal contradiction over whether any attendance-specific certificate exists at all).

## 8. Support Section

Open support via the floating chat button or the Support navbar tab. Under Help, select your batch and category. For General Query, describe your issue and send. For Lecture, Assignment, Evaluation, or Resource, select the specific item, confirm it, pick a common question (or write your own), then send. Track tickets under the My Tickets tab. For resolved tickets, rate the resolution (👍 / 👎). A 👎 can reopen or escalate the ticket if the issue was not solved.

For unresolved or unanswered issues, use Request a Callback or book a 1:1 Support slot if availabale in your batch.

Shortcut: On a lecture, assignment, evaluation, or resource detail page, use Raise Ticket button — this opens support already scoped to that item (skips category and item selection).

**Response-time commitment: 24-hour turnaround (TAT).** This wasn't found stated to students anywhere in ticket history or the canned-response library, but it is the confirmed internal target — safe to state directly if a student asks how long a reply should take.

## 12. Refer & Earn

Students can find the “Refer & Earn” button in the top navigation bar of the LMS. Clicking it takes them to the referral page, where they can access and share their unique referral link.

Students can earn up to ₹15,000 per successfully enrolled referral (referred friends get a flat 10% discount), shareable via WhatsApp/Telegram/LinkedIn/Instagram or a direct link, with an Impact Dashboard tracking the funnel (Sign Up → Finished Counselling → Registration → Full Fee Paid → Rewards Earned).

**Reward disbursement timeline: 30–45 days is the standard/ideal figure to quote.** Older records mentioned faster numbers for some payout methods (5–7 days, 1–2 weeks, or an immediate voucher) — those may reflect faster paths for specific payment methods, but 30–45 days is the confirmed baseline to give a student by default.

## 17. Video Playback and Recording Access

Recordings are accessed via **Learn > Lectures**, with quality selection (Auto, 360p–1080p). If playback is buffering, reduce quality, switch browser, or clear cache. Recordings are typically available within about 24 hours of the live session — if one is missing well beyond that, raise a ticket under **Product Support > Lecture**.

**System requirements:** the latest version of **Google Chrome**, on **Windows 10/11 or the latest macOS**.

**Recommended laptop/PC specs:**

- _Basic:_ 1.6 GHz processor, 4–8 GB RAM, 120 GB storage, a working webcam and microphone.
- _Recommended:_ 2 GHz+ processor, 8 GB RAM, 256 GB storage (preferably SSD).

**Internet:** a stable Wi-Fi/broadband connection, roughly 2–6 GB of daily data usage, **10 Mbps minimum / 20 Mbps recommended** download speed.

## 18. Zoom Integration and Live Sessions

Zoom connection status is managed at **Profile > Zoom Integrations**. For persistent Zoom authentication issues: check the integration status, reconnect if disconnected, confirm the Zoom email matches the LMS-registered email, then raise a ticket under **Product Support > Zoom/nolan Authentication** if it continues.

## 19. How to Use the Support System Effectively

Raise a ticket via **Support > Help**, selecting batch, category, and subcategory. Track existing tickets under **Raised Tickets**. For an unresolved or unanswered ticket, book a one-on-one support slot — that's the standard escalation path.

**One-on-one mentoring / doubt-clearing sessions are not available for every program.** Some programs have an active mentor-booking link (shared via LMS/email); where one doesn't exist, the standard alternative is Tutorial/Doubt-Resolution sessions plus the LMS Discussion forum — which are available to everyone regardless of program. Check whether the student's specific program has a booking link before answering with a flat yes or no.

## 20. Understanding Your Attendance on the LMS

Attendance status appears on the Lectures list (green = Present, red = Absent) and on each lecture's detail page, with a tooltip breakdown of Live vs. Recording status.

**How attendance is recorded:**

- **Live sessions:** join through the official Zoom link via LMS, using the registered (Nolan-authenticated) email, at least 5 minutes before start, and remain present for the **full session — a minimum of 90% of the duration** is the most commonly used threshold, though this is confirmed to vary by program (one program, "Model 3," uses 65% instead) — check the specific program before stating a number with certainty. Only faculty/academic sessions and Industry Mentor sessions count; **tutorials, Doubt-Resolution sessions, masterclasses, and Helpdesk sessions do not count** toward the attendance percentage either way.
- **Recordings:** if a student misses the live session, watching the **complete recording — without skipping or fast-forwarding — within 30 days** of the session marks them Present. Partial viewing, or watching after the 30-day window, does not count. Attendance updates take up to about 24 hours to reflect after either method.
- **There is no formal leave-request or attendance-pause policy.** If a student asks for leave (medical, family emergency, exams, work), there's no approval workflow or documentation process — the standard response acknowledges the situation, recommends catching up via recordings and tutorial sessions, and reminds the student they still need to meet the normal attendance threshold on their own. In practice, agents sometimes grant a softer, informal exception by marking one **specific session** as optional for that student (so it counts toward neither present nor absent) when there's a documented reason — this isn't a multi-day leave grant, just a per-session judgment call, and it's not guaranteed.
- **CGPA is not directly affected by attendance** in most programs — CGPA is primarily driven by evaluations and projects. Attendance instead gates the completion **certificate** itself (see §26 for the specific per-program thresholds), and may separately affect placement eligibility in some programs (unconfirmed generally — see §30).
- **Correcting a discrepancy:** A known technical cause is the Zoom “joined as Guest” issue, where the registered email may not be captured even though the student attended. If available, the student may share a screenshot from the session as supporting proof. After an attendance correction, if the update is still not visible, ask the student to log out, clear their browser cache, and log back in.
- Foundation/pre-requisite session attendance does not count toward the main program's attendance percentage at all (see §27).

## 21. Navigating Evaluations and Assessments

Evaluations appear in **Learn > Assignments**, marked with an orange "Evaluation" tag, and may include Objective, Subjective, and Coding components.

**Attempts and rescheduling:** Evaluation schedules are fixed — preponing or postponing is not permitted. Most evaluations provide **two scheduled attempts**; a student uses one or both, and the **higher score is retained**. No further rescheduling or reattempt is offered beyond the two built-in attempts (this pattern also covers most "I missed my evaluation" situations).

**Re-evaluation / score disputes:** re-evaluation based on disagreeing with how an answer was graded is **not permitted** as standing policy. If a student believes a specific question or the answer key is wrong, they can raise it with the evaluation name/date, the question, and their reasoning — this goes to a curriculum committee for review, which may result in a correction being issued generally, not a personal re-score. Separately, if the issue is a **genuine calculation or system error** (e.g. a question incorrectly marked "not attempted" due to a technical glitch, or an arithmetic mistake in totaling a score), that does get corrected on verification — this isn't "re-evaluation," it's fixing a mistake, and shouldn't be refused with the same "not permitted" line.

**Malpractice and proctoring:** all evaluations are proctored, typically with a **dual-device setup** — a laptop/desktop to attempt the exam, and a smartphone camera positioned to record the student's environment. Chrome permissions for camera, microphone, screen share, and window management are required. AI tools (ChatGPT, GitHub Copilot, or similar) are explicitly not permitted and count as plagiarism. Unauthorized materials or external help are not allowed unless explicitly stated for that exam; calculators/rough sheets are allowed only where the specific exam says so (some offline exams provide a calculator directly on the portal, so students don't need to bring one). **The plagiarism/malpractice policy is standard across programs** — confirmed — and follows a two-strike structure: first offense is a zero score plus a written warning; second offense is a zero score plus no placement assistance and no completion certificate, reviewed by a Disciplinary Committee with the student given a chance to explain first.

**Technical/network issues during an exam:** the two built-in attempts (above) exist partly to absorb situations like a technical failure, and a demo/mock evaluation is provided in advance so students can test their camera, mic, and connection beforehand. Because of this, additional remedies for technical issues during the actual evaluation are generally not provided — though a student can still raise a ticket with evidence for a genuinely critical issue, reviewed case-by-case.

**Syllabus and format:** evaluation syllabus, pattern, and weightage are communicated to students by **email**, on a scheduled basis ahead of each evaluation — there isn't a self-serve syllabus page to point students to. For **online** evaluations, information is shared **7 days prior**. For **offline** evaluations: students submit a city/state preference about a month in advance, the exam center is allocated based on availability, and the **admit card is issued 15 days prior** to the exam, with center details and any item restrictions.

**Results:** MCQ-based results may take up to 24 hours to appear; coding-question results can take up to about 7 days. Some end-of-module or ceremony-linked evaluations take longer (a few weeks) before results and a formal scorecard are released via LMS/email, pending institute confirmation.

**Mock evaluations** don't carry marks and don't count toward CGPA — they exist to familiarize students with the format and confirm their system setup works.

## 27. Pre-requisite / Foundation Stage Policy

Foundation ("pre-requisite") classes run before the official Main Batch start, to build background understanding. They are **optional**: attendance and any foundation-stage assignments do **not** affect the main program's attendance percentage, CGPA, or certification — regardless of whether a student attends, skips, or joins the batch late. Foundation-stage assignments are practice-only and can be submitted late without consequence. Schedule and start-date updates for the foundation phase are communicated by email and LMS announcement shortly before they begin.

**Foundation-session recordings are available** to a student who joined late or missed them — they can access these recordings on the portal to catch up at their own pace. (This resolves an earlier internal contradiction where one canned reply said recordings weren't available; the confirmed answer is that they are.)

## 28. Student Kit Policy

Kit contents and whether a physical kit is sent at all **vary by program/batch** — not every program includes one. Where a kit is sent, it typically includes an ID card and admission-related documents.

**Shipping timeline (confirmed):** once the course starts, the student kit takes **15–20 working days** — this is the standard figure to quote; an earlier "up to 30 days" reference for the tracking-ID step specifically is superseded by this. The recommended process is to share the tracking ID with the student and only close the ticket once that's been provided, rather than closing it earlier.

Students have a **24-hour window** after the soft copy is shared to submit or correct their kit form (address, name, T-shirt size, etc.) — after that window closes, the details are locked in for kit processing and **cannot be changed**, including T-shirt size.

For a wrong, missing, or damaged kit item (only usable once the kit has already been delivered), students can raise a request via [this form](https://forms.gle/AxDJXAt7w5i79crPA). For a wrong-route/misdelivery specifically, a replacement is arranged with a 15–20 day window.

## 29. Administrative and Financial Requests

- **Fee receipt / GST invoice:** raise a support ticket with enrollment details; the finance team responds within **3–5 working days**. Some programs also show this directly under a Payments/Receipts section on the LMS.
- **Bonafide letter / proof of enrollment:** same process, raise a ticket stating the specific requirement and purpose; **3–5 working days**.
- **Payment methods:** fees are paid **before** a student gets LMS access — there is no post-enrollment invoicing flow through support. Three payment options exist: **full payment**, **partial payment**, and **EMI**. (Exact installment schedule/dates for partial/EMI plans still need to be confirmed if a student asks for specifics.)
- **Refunds:** the default is **no refund** once a student is enrolled and the course has started — fees, including active EMIs, remain the student's obligation regardless of continuation. A refund may still be considered in the case of a genuinely **severe issue**, reviewed case-by-case — don't state "no refund, ever, no exceptions" as an absolute. Cancelling admission is not processed as a standard request. Note also that students sometimes phrase these requests unclearly in a ticket — a Program Coordinator call to understand the actual situation is a normal and expected part of handling this, not a sign the request is being mishandled.
- **Deferral to a later batch:** not automatic, but a student can request it by emailing their Program Coordinator; reviewed case-by-case, subject to institute approval and seat availability (the curriculum of a later batch may differ from the current one).
- **Batch, language, or course/specialization change:** not processed as a routine request once a program has started. The only path is an emailed exception request to the Program Coordinator, reviewed against seat availability and other batches' progress; changing course/specialization may also involve a fee adjustment.
- **Campus immersion:** an in-person component (workshops, campus tour, networking, guest lectures), typically 2–3 days around a long weekend, announced about a month ahead by email/LMS. **Travel and accommodation are the student's own expense.** For most programs the certificate isn't withheld if a student can't attend; for at least one program the certificate is specifically handed out at the immersion event, so a non-attending student there needs a separate arrangement — confirm before stating this definitively for that program.
